Holosun 507C X2 — The Red Dot Everyone's Running For Good Reason

Marcus Webb June 18, 2026 · 8 min read · Optics
[ Cover photo — Holosun 507C X2 mounted on pistol, studio lighting ]

Overview

The Holosun 507C X2 has become the default recommendation for anyone building a red-dot equipped pistol in the $250–$300 range. After running one for over 2,000 rounds across three different hosts — a Glock 17, a P320 X-Carry, and a Shadow Systems MR920 — I can tell you exactly when it earns that reputation and where it falls short.

Holosun's Multi-Reticle System (MRS) gives you the choice between a 2 MOA dot, a 32 MOA circle, or both together. In practice, most shooters land on circle-dot and stay there. The solar failsafe cell on top means you almost never touch the battery.

Build quality and footprint

The 507C X2 uses a 6061 aluminum housing with a titanium top plate — noticeably more solid than the plastic-feeling SRO at double the price. The shake-awake feature activates the optic on any motion, so you draw to a live dot every time. Standby battery life is rated at 50,000 hours; real-world I've seen over two years between changes even running it manually most of the time.

Footprint is the standard Holosun K pattern, which fits most optics-ready slides without a plate. On the Glock MOS, you'll want the appropriate adapter. The screws are T10 torx — carry a spare on any long range day.

Glass and reticle clarity

The lens is green-tinted glass, not the clearer glass you get in the 509T or the Trijicon RMR. In direct sunlight at low brightness settings the dot can wash out slightly. At settings 6–8 it's bright enough for any outdoor use. Indoors, settings 3–4 are plenty. The 32 MOA circle is crisp with no visible pixelation at arm's length.

Astigmatism sufferers: the dot can appear as a starburst depending on your prescription. This is an optic limitation, not a defect. Try a 3 MOA dot variant if the 2 MOA gives you trouble.

Durability

IP67 rated. I've shot it in heavy rain with no issues, and it's survived a couple of unintentional drops from bench height. The X2 generation added a larger, recessed adjustment cap versus the original 507C — the old design was notorious for breaking if torqued too hard. The new caps are significantly more durable. Zero retention after 500 rounds of +P has been rock solid on all three hosts tested.

Mounting and co-witness

The 507C X2 sits lower than competitors like the Trijicon RMR, which gives you a lower 1/3 co-witness with most suppressor-height sights. Full co-witness requires taller sights. If you're switching from an RMR footprint slide, confirm the plate compatibility — they don't share mounting patterns despite the similar size.

Who it's for

  • First-time red dot shooter who wants a reliable optic without spending RMR money
  • Carry builds where solar failsafe battery life matters
  • Competition shooters running USPSA Production/Carry Optics who want a proven platform
  • Anyone who wants MRS flexibility — the circle-dot combo is genuinely useful for fast target acquisition

Bottom line

At $289, the 507C X2 is the honest answer to "what red dot should I start with?" It's not the clearest glass on the market and it's not as bombproof as a Trijicon RMR, but it's 85% of the optic at 55% of the price with features — solar cell, MRS, shake-awake — the RMR still doesn't have. If you're already spending $500+ on a slide cut, spend the $289 here and don't look back.
