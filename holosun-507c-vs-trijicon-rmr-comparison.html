Holosun 507C X2 vs Trijicon RMR Type 2 — Which One Actually Wins?

Marcus Webb June 10, 2026 · 10 min read · Optics
Our Pick
Holosun 507C X2
Better features, solar failsafe, multi-reticle system, and proven durability — at nearly half the price of the RMR.
$289
[ Cover photo — Holosun 507C X2 and Trijicon RMR side by side on bench ]

The Trijicon RMR has been the default answer to "what red dot should I put on my pistol?" for over a decade. The Holosun 507C X2 is the first optic to seriously challenge that. Both are on hundreds of thousands of pistols. Both get used for carry, competition, and duty. So which one actually wins when you put them side by side?

I ran both back-to-back on the same Glock 17 MOS for six months — approximately 3,000 rounds total, a prolonged rain session, a torture-test drop series, and a long-term zero retention check. Here's the full breakdown.

Glass and dot quality

This is the RMR's clearest advantage. Trijicon's glass is visibly cleaner — less tint, sharper dot edge, and better performance in transitional lighting (bright room to dark hallway). If you have astigmatism, the RMR dot will look cleaner than the Holosun's. The 507C X2's green-tinted glass is fine for 95% of uses but shows its limitation at range.

The RMR's 3.25 MOA dot is also larger and easier to acquire under stress — a point that matters more than it sounds for carry guns.

Durability: closer than you'd think

The RMR's reputation is built on torture tests from the military contract era. It's passed MIL-STD-810G, survived being driven over by vehicles, and is the optic of choice for MARSOC. That legacy is real.

That said — the Holosun 507C X2 is not a fragile optic. In my drop tests (concrete from 4 feet, 10 drops each), both optics held zero within one click. Both survived submersion. The Holosun housing is aluminum-plus-titanium; it's not going to fold under normal duty use. For a civilian shooter — even a hard-use one — the durability gap is smaller than the price gap.

Footprint and slide compatibility

The RMR footprint is the universal standard. Almost every optics-ready slide in production accepts RMR pattern directly — no adapter plate needed. The Holosun 507C X2 uses the wider Holosun K footprint, which requires an adapter plate on many slides. If you're changing slides or buying a factory-milled gun, verify compatibility before ordering.

This is a practical consideration that tips the scales toward the RMR for duty or professional use where equipment changes happen frequently.

Features the RMR simply doesn't have

The 507C X2's solar failsafe is genuinely useful — the optic can run indefinitely in outdoor environments without touching the battery. For a carry gun, this means less maintenance anxiety. The shake-awake feature ensures you always draw to a live optic. The multi-reticle system lets you run a 32 MOA circle for fast close-range acquisition and switch to a 2 MOA dot for precision work. The RMR has none of these.

Verdict

For most shooters, the Holosun 507C X2 is the right call. It's $230 cheaper, has better features, and is durable enough for any civilian application. The RMR's advantages — glass clarity, lighter weight, universal footprint, and war-proven toughness — matter most to professionals running optics on duty guns with strict equipment standards, or to precision shooters who will notice the glass difference at distance.

If you're building a carry gun, competition rig, or range pistol and you're not already committed to an RMR-pattern slide: buy the Holosun.
